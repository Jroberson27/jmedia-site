Why a retainer instead of a one off shoot

One shoot is a moment. A month is a story.

A single video gets posted, does its work in a week, and then the feed moves on. Businesses that stay visible are the ones putting something out consistently, and that is far easier to plan than to improvise.

You stop paying to re explain your business

Every new production starts with someone learning who you are. On a retainer that happens once. By month two I already know your space, your people, and how you want to sound.

Predictable cost, predictable output

A fixed monthly number you can budget against, and an agreed number of finished pieces. No quoting every time you need something.

The same person every time

I shoot, edit, and deliver everything myself. That is why the look holds together across months instead of drifting between freelancers.

How It Works

Four steps, every month

01

Plan

We start the month with a short call to agree themes, locations, and what needs capturing.

02

Shoot

I run the day myself with my own gear. No crew to coordinate, nobody new to brief.

03

Edit

I cut, color, and mix in house, which is how the look stays consistent month to month.

04

Deliver

Files sized for where they are going. Vertical for social, wide for the site, ready to post.

Before You Commit

Start with a pilot. No long contract to find out if this works.

  • Pilot first. One location, one session, $150 to $250. You get 2 to 3 finished short form videos and a handful of photos. It covers my time and gear, and it is not a discounted retainer.
  • Then month to month for the first three. If the pilot goes well we run three months before anyone signs anything longer.
  • Limited slots. I shoot on set for theme park and national brand productions, so I take on a small number of retainer clients at a time to keep delivery dates real.
